04 / EXACT PARTIALS, INCLUDING ZERO MARGINALS.
Differentiate the function.
Not a sample.
Each gradient averages finite differences over contexts excluding its item. Each mixed partial averages second differences over contexts excluding both items. This avoids division by x or 1-x and remains defined at zero and one. Diagonal Hessian entries are zero.
Nonpositive mixed partials follow from submodularity, not from a generic set function. One item has no pair; its selected pair/context witnesses are explicitly null.


05 / TWO ENDPOINTS. ONE REPLAYABLE DECISION.
Exchange mass.
Keep the count.
At each step choose the lowest-index fractional pair i,j. Move along x+t(e_i-e_j) until a coordinate becomes zero or one. Evaluate both endpoints exactly, take the larger value, and choose the left endpoint on ties. Integral coordinates stay fixed. This deterministic choice does not preserve initial inclusion marginals.
Why the value cannot fall when submodular
The swap polynomial is F(x) + (∂iF-∂jF)t - ∂ijF t². Its curvature is -2∂ijF ≥ 0. The current t=0 lies between the two endpoints, so at least one endpoint has value at least F(x). Sum preservation and an integer total rule out exactly one remaining fractional coordinate. Each step fixes at least one coordinate. No optimality or new approximation ratio follows from an arbitrary supplied starting point.




06 / FINITE. EXACT. NO INFLATED CLAIM.
A witness,
not an optimizer.
The first control has probabilities [1/2,1/2] and k=1. Expected count is 1, but P(count=2)=1/4. Duplicate coverage has expected value 3/4; either rounded singleton has value 1. A synergy function is not submodular and can have both endpoints worse, so rounding is withheld. A nonmonotone cut is submodular and still rounds: monotonicity and normalization are not required for this swap proof.
Complete enumeration is exponential. No continuous greedy, dependent-randomized rounding, marginal-preservation, concentration, cost/knapsack, matroid, model training or downstream data-selection result.
