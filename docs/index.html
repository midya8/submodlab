05 / THE BOUND HAS CONDITIONS
A certificate,
with its assumptions.
The finite-k factor is 1 − (1 − 1/k)k for k > 0, at least 1 − 1/e. It is a worst-case guarantee for normalized, nonnegative, monotone submodular objectives under a uniform cardinality constraint. It is not a measured score and does not claim greedy is optimal.
At each prefix A, every feasible optimum is at most f(A) plus the sum of the k largest nonnegative remaining marginals, also capped by f(V). It is k, not k − |A|: the unknown optimum need not contain A. The minimum bound across visited prefixes is a valid upper bound. If any assumption fails, all theorem and upper-bound fields are null. Zero denominators produce undefined ratios, not a perfect score.


06 / CHECK EVERY LOCAL INEQUALITY
Diminishing returns.
Or a counterexample.
Every absent-item edge checks f(A+j) − f(A) ≥ 0. Every unordered absent pair checks f(A+i) + f(A+j) − f(A) − f(A+i+j) ≥ 0. These local pair inequalities are equivalent to full finite-set submodularity. Complete failure lists stay visible, not repaired.

07 / EXACT ARITHMETIC. BOUNDED AMBITION.
Six items.
No inflated claim.
One to six selectable items, at most 64 subsets. Facility matrices have one to eight represented rows. Input cells are integer/fraction strings, at most 12 characters; reduced numerator magnitude and denominator ≤ 10,000. Table mode is a complete finite value oracle, not a claim that arbitrary set functions have a greedy approximation guarantee.
No cost/knapsack, matroid, lazy/stochastic greedy, learned similarity, corpus, downstream metric or performance benchmark. Scaling all supplied values by a positive factor preserves choices and ratios but does not validate their real-world meaning.
